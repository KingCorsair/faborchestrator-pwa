FabOrchestrator PWA Business Phases

What the app will let people do, and when. Delivery runs in six phases: the first week proves the groundwork, and every phase after that switches something on that a person can actually use — signing in and talking to FabOrchestrator, asking anything from the front page, real plant answers on a phone, dashboards, and finally an app the business can rely on. Each row says what becomes possible, what we can show you, and the week it is ready. The small WP codes just point to the matching detail in the engineering plan.

PHASE 0Foundation and proven link None yet, deliberately. What this phase buys is certainty: one codebase anyone on the team can build, written evidence of how the live platform actually behaves, and the app’s ability to receive an answer while it is being written rather than after it is finished. User can do: nothing new — this is the phase that stops the project discovering an environment problem in week four. WP0WP6WP3 Baseline & environment validation, live answer streaming, platform client layer. A clean checkout that builds; a one-page report answering the open questions about the live environment with evidence; a recorded exchange showing FabOrchestrator delivering an answer progressively. PHASE 1Secure access to FabOrchestrator A named person can reach FabOrchestrator through the app and hold a live conversation, under their own account and their own permissions. The credential stays on our server and never reaches the handset, so every question is attributable and every answer respects that person’s data access, limits and audit trail. User can do: sign in and ask FabOrchestrator a question through the app, watching the answer arrive as it is written. WP2WP4 Sign-in & session security, secure connector. A live conversation with FabOrchestrator, signed in as a real user, shown end to end — with the platform credential demonstrably absent from the phone. Week 23.5 days of work M1 ✓B1 ✓
PHASE 2Usable and agentic conversations A user asks for what they want from the landing page and the system works out where the request belongs — which agent, which tools, which data — then does the work and returns the answer. Follow-up questions stay in the same thread. Choosing an agent by name remains available as a direct path for anyone who prefers it. User can do: type a question without knowing the product’s internal structure, and get it answered by the right part of the platform. WP5WP7WP13 Conversation handling, agent selection & access control, and the new agentic entry point. A plant question typed on the landing screen with nothing selected, answered by the right agent and labelled with which one served it; the route changed in one tap; a multi-question conversation held; every agent still directly reachable, and the permission-restricted one still explaining itself on its card. PHASE 3Real plant value on mobile A supervisor installs the app from a QR code and asks real MES and quality questions from the floor — yield, downtime, scrap — and reads the answer as a table on their phone. No app store, no IT deployment. This is the phase the business case rests on. User can do: get a plant answer, on a phone, standing next to the line it is about. WP1WP8 Mobile app foundation, live plant data answers. Installed on one iPhone and one Android handset from a QR code; a yield or downtime question answered from live MES data and read as a table on the phone itself. Week 33.0 days of work M3 ✓B3 ✓
PHASE 4Rich operational output Answers stop being only text. Dashboards and charts the platform generates open full-screen on the phone; a long-running question shows what it is doing rather than sitting silent; and anything that goes wrong explains itself in plain language with a next step and a reference support can trace. User can do: ask for a chart and get one, and know what to do when the answer cannot be produced. WP9WP10 Dashboard & visual output, progress & failure handling. A dashboard requested and rendered on the handset, proven isolated from the app around it; each identified failure — expired session, data connection down, platform busy — producing its own message and next step. Phases run in this order because the dependencies require it: nothing can reach FabOrchestrator before sign-in works, automatic routing needs working agents to route to, and plant data cannot be proven before the connection is proven. B1–B5 are business milestones, each closing a phase; they sit alongside the technical milestones M0–M5 in the engineering plan rather than replacing them. Phase 0 has no business milestone because it delivers certainty, not capability. Each phase is dated by the one week in which it is finished, assuming one engineer at five days a week: the running total of work reaches 4.0 days inside week 1, 7.5 by week 2, 11.0 and then 14.0 during week 3, 19.0 in week 4, and 24.5 in week 5. Phases 2 and 3 both finish in week 3 because Phase 3 is small. The days shown are the scheduled figures including probe and demonstration time, which is why they total 24.5 rather than the 23.0 of build effort alone; with two engineers the phases overlap and delivery compresses to about 4 weeks. Phase 1 is the decision point — the whole architecture is proven there, about a fifth of the way into the budget, not at the end.
